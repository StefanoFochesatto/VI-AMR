QUESTION

Search for papers which apply adaptive mesh refinement to variational
inequalities and obstacle problems, but via methods that should work for
degenerate elliptic operators, e.g. porous media equations and the glacier
geometry problem; the real question is whether the existing a posteriori
estimators extend beyond the uniformly-elliptic classical obstacle problem
which is only a model problem.


ANSWER

The classical theory (uniformly elliptic, potential operators)
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Chen & Nochetto (2000), "Residual type a posteriori error estimates for
  elliptic obstacle problems," Numer. Math. 84 -- the original BR-style
  residual estimator, built on a positivity-preserving interpolant. This is
  what VIAMR's brinactivemark is descended from.
- Nochetto, Siebert & Veeser (2003), pointwise/localized a posteriori
  control (Numer. Math.) -- the NSV method.
- Braess, "A posteriori error estimators for obstacle problems -- another
  look" -- a survey making explicit what these proofs actually lean on:
  coercivity/continuity constants of a linear, self-adjoint bilinear form,
  plus a complementarity/duality-gap argument that needs the operator to be
  a potential (gradient of a convex functional).

Both load-bearing assumptions -- linearity/uniform ellipticity and
potentiality -- are exactly what fail for the porous.py operator and the
tilted SIA operator in glacier/METHOD.md.

Attempts at extension, and where each one actually stops
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. p-Laplacian PDE, no obstacle (Liu & Yan 2001/2002; Ebmeyer & Liu 2005,
   "quasi-norm" a priori/a posteriori theory) -- shows that naively reusing
   an energy-norm residual estimator on a degenerate operator gives
   estimates that are not equivalent to the true error as p moves away from
   2; a quasi-norm (weighted by |grad u|^(p-2)-type factors) is needed to
   restore reliability/efficiency. This is the closest theoretical
   explanation for what porous.py's own comment already flags: "large BR
   eta values occur near the free boundary... for the ordinary Laplacian
   that would not be true." That is the quasi-norm mismatch showing up
   numerically.

2. p-Laplacian obstacle problem -- Banz & Stephan, "Higher order FEM for
   the obstacle problem of the p-Laplacian -- a variational inequality
   approach," Comput. Math. Appl. 76 (2018) 1639-1660, plus their earlier
   hp-IPDG estimator papers. This is the one genuine precedent for
   "BR/Chen-Nochetto-style estimator + obstacle constraint + degenerate
   operator," for p in (1, infinity). Still a potential operator (pure
   p-Laplacian, self-adjoint), so it doesn't touch the non-potentiality
   problem below.

3. Non-potential monotone operators -- Bobkov & Pastukhova (2025), "A
   posteriori estimates for problems with monotone operators," Sbornik:
   Mathematics (arXiv:2504.09931). Derives a posteriori estimates for VIs
   with monotone but non-potential operators, specifically avoiding duality
   theory, demonstrated on anisotropic/polyharmonic/fractional p-Laplacians.
   This matters because glacier/METHOD.md states outright that the tilted
   SIA operator (with Z = Phi(u)) is not the first-order condition for any
   minimization -- i.e., non-potential -- which is precisely the case
   Braess-style duality arguments cannot reach. Likely the highest-value
   read of the set.

4. Steady porous medium equation (the porous.py case,
   -div(u^m grad u) = f) with an obstacle constraint -- no dedicated paper
   found. What exists (Nochetto & Verdi, Chen & Nochetto on the two-phase
   Stefan problem) is a posteriori theory for the parabolic PME/Stefan
   free-boundary problem, a different setting (time-regularized degeneracy,
   not a steady obstacle VI). This looks like a genuine gap, consistent
   with porous.py's own comment flagging the naive reuse as an open
   question rather than citing a justification.

5. Glacier/SIA specifically -- Jouvet & Bueler (2012) establish
   well-posedness of the obstacle-problem formulation but derive no a
   posteriori estimator. The ISSM ice-sheet AMR paper (Ice Sheet System
   Model, GMD 2019) uses a Zienkiewicz-Zhu gradient-recovery estimator
   around the grounding line purely heuristically, with no proof it is
   reliable/efficient for this degenerate, non-potential operator -- same
   epistemic status as the GR estimator on porous.py/glacier.

Bottom line on "does the theory extend"
----------------------------------------------------------------
- For a single power-law degeneracy with a potential structure (plain
  p-Laplacian obstacle problem): partially yes, via Banz-Stephan, but even
  there the quasi-norm literature (item 1) suggests the naive energy-residual
  estimator is the wrong norm to trust as degeneracy strengthens --
  reliability/efficiency constants are not shown to stay bounded uniformly
  in p or gamma.
- For non-potential operators (tilted/doubly-degenerate SIA): essentially
  open. Bobkov-Pastukhova (2025) is the first plausible general framework,
  but nobody has applied it to a doubly-degenerate, solution-dependent-tilt
  operator like SIA.
- For the plain porous-medium obstacle problem (porous.py): no dedicated a
  posteriori theory found at all -- BR is being applied by analogy, and the
  inline comment already flags the qualitative symptom (anomalously large
  eta near the free boundary) that quasi-norm theory would predict.

So the honest framing: BR/NSV/GR as currently coded are heuristic
extensions, empirically validated (Jaccard agreement, convergence plots)
but not backed by reliability/efficiency proofs once uniform ellipticity
and potentiality are left behind -- and the Bobkov-Pastukhova and
Banz-Stephan papers are the two most promising anchors for building or
citing an actual theoretical bridge rather than relying purely on
numerical evidence.

----------------------------------------------------------------

  estimates for elliptic obstacle problems. Numer. Math. 84, 527-548.

  look. Numer. Math.

  SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 39, 40.

  for the piecewise linear finite element approximation of p-Laplacian
  problems. Numer. Math. 100, 233-258.

  problem of the p-Laplacian -- a variational inequality approach. Comput.
  Math. Appl. 76, 1639-1660.

  problems with monotone operators. Sbornik: Mathematics. arXiv:2504.09931.

  problem).

- Fochesatto, G.S. Adaptive Mesh Refinement for Variational Inequalities.
  arXiv:2502.14206 (the VIAMR paper itself -- describes VCES/UDO methods
  but does not address a posteriori theory for degenerate operators).
